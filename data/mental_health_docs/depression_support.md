# Understanding and Coping With Depression

## What Is Depression?
Depression (major depressive disorder) is a medical condition characterised by persistently low mood and/or loss of interest in activities, lasting at least two weeks, that significantly impairs daily functioning. It is not a character flaw, laziness, or something you can "snap out of." Depression involves real changes in brain chemistry, neural circuitry, and hormonal regulation.

Depression is the leading cause of disability worldwide (WHO). It is also highly treatable — more than 80% of people respond to treatment.

## Types of Depression
- **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** Episodes of severe symptoms that interfere with all aspects of life.
- **Persistent Depressive Disorder (Dysthymia):** Lower-grade but chronic depression lasting 2+ years.
- **Bipolar Depression:** Depressive episodes that alternate with mania or hypomania.
- **Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD):** Depression linked to seasonal light changes, typically winter.
- **Postpartum Depression:** Occurs after childbirth; more severe than "baby blues".

## Common Symptoms
**Emotional:**
- Persistent sad, empty, or hopeless mood
- Loss of interest or pleasure in activities once enjoyed (anhedonia)
- Feelings of worthlessness, guilt, or shame
- Irritability, frustration, or restlessness

**Physical:**
- Fatigue and low energy nearly every day
- Changes in sleep (insomnia or sleeping too much)
- Changes in appetite or weight
- Psychomotor changes (feeling slowed down or agitated)
- Unexplained physical complaints (headaches, digestive issues)

**Cognitive:**
- Difficulty concentrating, remembering, or making decisions
- Negative, pessimistic thinking
- Thoughts of death or suicide (requires immediate professional attention)

## Self-Care Strategies During Depression

### Behavioural Activation
Depression creates a vicious cycle: low mood → withdrawal → less reward → lower mood. **Behavioural activation** breaks this cycle by scheduling small, achievable activities that used to bring pleasure or meaning — even when motivation is absent. Start tiny: a 5-minute walk, making a cup of tea, texting a friend.

### Movement
Exercise is one of the most evidence-supported interventions for mild-to-moderate depression. Even 20–30 minutes of walking 3 times per week can produce clinically meaningful improvement by raising endorphins, BDNF (brain growth factor), and serotonin.

### Sleep Hygiene
Depression disrupts sleep, and poor sleep worsens depression.
- Go to bed and wake at consistent times, even weekends.
- Avoid screens for 1 hour before bed.
- Keep the bedroom cool, dark, and quiet.
- If you cannot sleep after 20 minutes, get up and do something calm until sleepy.

### Social Connection
Isolation amplifies depression. Make a point to maintain at least one social contact per day, even if it is just a text message. Let trusted people in your life know what you are experiencing.

### Limit Alcohol
Alcohol is a central nervous system depressant. While it may temporarily numb pain, it reliably worsens depression over time.

### Structure and Routine
Depression thrives in unstructured time. Create a gentle daily routine — fixed wake time, meals, and one small goal — to reduce decision fatigue and create forward momentum.

### Journaling
Write down three things you noticed today (not necessarily "good" things — just observations). Over time this trains attention away from exclusively negative focus.

## When to Seek Professional Help
See a doctor or mental health professional if:
- Depressive symptoms have lasted more than 2 weeks
- You are unable to function at work, school, or in relationships
- You are having thoughts of self-harm or suicide
- You are using substances to cope

**If you are having thoughts of suicide, please reach out immediately:**
- Call or text **988** (US Suicide & Crisis Lifeline)
- Text HOME to **741741** (Crisis Text Line)
- Go to your nearest emergency room

## Professional Treatment Options
- **Psychotherapy:** CBT, Interpersonal Therapy (IPT), and Behavioural Activation Therapy are all evidence-based.
- **Medication:** Antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs) work for many people. It may take 4–6 weeks to see full effects and sometimes a few trials to find the right medication.
- **Combined Treatment:** Therapy + medication is more effective than either alone for moderate-to-severe depression.
- **Other Options:** Light therapy (for SAD), exercise therapy, TMS (transcranial magnetic stimulation) for treatment-resistant depression.

## Supporting Someone With Depression
- Listen without trying to fix.
- Avoid "just cheer up" or "others have it worse."
- Offer specific help ("I'll pick up groceries Tuesday — does that work?").
- Encourage — gently and consistently — professional help.
- Take care of your own wellbeing too.

## Key Reminder
Depression lies. It tells you nothing will help, that you are a burden, that things will never improve. These are symptoms of the illness — not truth. With appropriate treatment, most people with depression recover and go on to live full, meaningful lives.
