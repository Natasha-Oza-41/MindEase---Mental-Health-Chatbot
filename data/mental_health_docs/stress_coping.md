# Stress: Understanding It and Building Resilience

## What Is Stress?
Stress is the body's physiological and psychological response to demands that exceed perceived resources. The stress response (fight-or-flight) is mediated by the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and releases cortisol and adrenaline. This response evolved to handle short-term physical threats.

**The problem:** modern stressors (deadlines, financial pressure, relationship conflict) are chronic and do not resolve quickly, causing prolonged HPA activation that harms the body and mind.

## Stress vs Anxiety: Key Difference
- **Stress** is typically tied to an identifiable external cause. It often resolves when the situation changes.
- **Anxiety** persists even when the stressor is removed or absent, and is driven more by internal thought patterns.

## Physical Effects of Chronic Stress
- Elevated blood pressure and heart disease risk
- Impaired immune function (increased susceptibility to illness)
- Digestive problems (IBS, acid reflux)
- Muscle tension, headaches, jaw clenching
- Sleep disruption
- Weight gain (elevated cortisol promotes fat storage)
- Memory and concentration difficulties

## Identifying Your Stress Signals
Everyone responds to stress differently. Learn your personal early warning signs:
- Physical: tight shoulders, shallow breathing, headaches, GI symptoms
- Emotional: irritability, tearfulness, feeling overwhelmed, cynicism
- Behavioural: procrastination, social withdrawal, overeating, increased screen time
- Cognitive: difficulty concentrating, racing thoughts, forgetfulness

## Coping Strategies

### Problem-Focused Coping (address the source)
Use when the stressor is within your control.
- **Time blocking:** Assign specific time slots to specific tasks. Protect them.
- **Prioritisation:** Use the Eisenhower Matrix (Urgent/Important quadrants) to decide what needs your attention first.
- **Saying no:** Overcommitment is a primary driver of chronic stress. Declining requests is a skill, not a failure.
- **Break large tasks down:** A huge project feels paralyzing; a single next action feels manageable.

### Emotion-Focused Coping (manage your response)
Use when the stressor is outside your control.
- **Diaphragmatic breathing:** Slow, deep belly breathing directly counteracts the stress response by activating the vagus nerve and parasympathetic system.
- **Physical exercise:** Metabolises stress hormones. Even a 10-minute walk helps.
- **Journaling:** Write about the stressor — what happened, how you feel, what you can and cannot control.
- **Talking to someone:** Social support is one of the strongest buffers against the harmful effects of stress.
- **Mindfulness:** Being fully present in the moment prevents the future-focused "what if" thinking that amplifies stress.

### Restorative Practices
- **Sleep:** Sleep deprivation increases cortisol and makes stress harder to cope with. Prioritise 7–9 hours.
- **Nature exposure:** Even 20 minutes outdoors in green spaces measurably reduces cortisol.
- **Creative activities:** Drawing, music, cooking — activities that engage flow states reduce stress hormones.
- **Digital detox periods:** News and social media are significant sources of chronic background stress.

## The STOP Technique (Quick Stress Reset)
- **S** — Stop what you are doing.
- **T** — Take a slow, deep breath.
- **O** — Observe: what are you feeling physically? Emotionally? What thoughts are present?
- **P** — Proceed with intention, rather than reactivity.

## Building Long-Term Stress Resilience
Resilience is not toughness — it is the ability to recover from adversity. It is built through:
- **Consistent self-care:** Sleep, nutrition, movement, connection.
- **Meaningful relationships:** People with strong social support tolerate stress better.
- **Sense of purpose:** Having reasons beyond survival reduces how threatening stressors feel.
- **Cognitive flexibility:** Reframing stressors as challenges rather than threats (this is trainable).
- **Seeking help early:** Addressing stress before it becomes burnout or breakdown.

## When Stress Becomes a Medical Concern
See a doctor if:
- Stress is causing physical symptoms (chest pain, persistent headaches, GI problems)
- You cannot function at work or in relationships
- You are using alcohol, substances, or other unhealthy behaviours to cope
- You feel consistently hopeless or overwhelmed for more than a few weeks

## Key Reminder
No one is immune to stress, including the most high-functioning and successful people. Managing it is a learnable skill, not an innate trait. Small, consistent practices compound into significant resilience over time.
